Why children's frames are a different product

A child's frame fails for reasons an adult frame never does. It is dropped, sat on, twisted out of shape in a school bag, and pulled off one-handed. The result is that hinge screws back out, acetate goes brittle at the bridge, and the temple arms lose their alignment within a term. This is why the kids category is dominated by TR90 rather than acetate: the material is a thermoplastic polyamide that flexes roughly 30 to 40 degrees before it returns to shape, so a frame that is bent in a bag comes back rather than snapping.

Every style in this section is built on that logic, in small-fit dimensions rather than an adult frame scaled down in a photograph.

Sizing that actually fits a child

The critical numbers are the temple length and the frame width, not the lens shape. A frame that is 8 to 10 mm too wide slides down the nose all day, and the child pushes it back up with a finger on the lens, which is how most scratched lenses happen. Kids frames here run short temples with a wrap at the tip so the arm grips behind the ear instead of resting on it.

Weight matters as much as width. The TR90 styles in this range sit at 45 to 60 grams, light enough that a primary-school child stops noticing the frame after the first hour.

Blue-light lenses for screen use

The demand driver in this category is screen time, not prescription. A blue-light lens filters the short-wavelength band around 415 to 455 nm, which is the part of the visible spectrum most associated with eye strain during close work. For a wholesale order the practical question is the coating specification and whether the lens carries a visible tint: a clear low-reflection coating sells into schools, a light-yellow tint signals the function on a retail shelf.

These are non-prescription lenses as catalogued. If your order needs prescription-ready frames, we cut them to your lens supplier's pattern rather than shipping assembled.

Ordering for a school or clinic programme

Kids programmes buy in size runs rather than in colour runs: a school order is usually two or three sizes across one or two colours, with the logo on the temple or on a soft case. Minimum order is one piece per style for sampling, and the volume ladder starts at 100 pieces. Custom colour matching requires a Pantone reference and roughly 15 days on top of the standard lead time.

Frequently asked

Answers cover the questions we are asked most about this range. Anything else, ask us directly.

What age range do these frames fit?

They are cut for roughly 4 to 12 years, in two size groups. The frame width is the number to check against your market rather than the age label.

Are the lenses prescription-ready?

The catalogued lenses are non-prescription blue-light lenses. Frames can be supplied unglazed so your own lab cuts and fits prescription lenses.

Why TR90 instead of acetate for children?

TR90 flexes and returns to shape, so a frame bent in a school bag survives. Acetate is stiffer and cracks at the bridge under the same treatment.

What is the minimum order?

One piece per style for a sample, with the volume price ladder starting at 100 pieces. Mixed colours are allowed within the same style.

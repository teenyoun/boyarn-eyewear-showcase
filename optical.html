What we produce

Our optical range is built for two buyers: the private label brand that wants a recognisable shape with its own logo, and the wholesaler supplying opticians who needs a dependable, re-orderable basic. The catalogue runs from thin metal rectangles and squares through acetate and TR90 shapes to titanium rimless and semi-rimless. Blue-light blocking versions of the core shapes are the fastest moving part of the range, because the screen-time segment keeps buying.

Every frame here is designed to be glazed. That sounds like a given, but it is the single most common failure point in cheap optical frames: the rim groove is too shallow, the temple screws strip, or the frame will not hold a lens under stress. Ours are built from stock moulds already running in production, so the geometry is proven.

Frame materials and construction

Acetate gives the best colour depth and the most premium hand-feel, and it can be adjusted with heat, which opticians value when fitting. It is heavier than the alternatives, so acetate suits the higher price points and full-rim shapes.

TR90 is light, flexible and impact resistant, which makes it the default for thin full-rim frames and for anything aimed at younger or active wearers. It holds colour well in matt finishes and keeps its shape under daily handling.

Metal frames are built on stainless steel or alloy with adjustable nose arms. Titanium appears in rimless and ultra-light designs where weight is the whole proposition. On metal and combination frames, the hinge and the nose pad arms are where failures show first, so specification there matters more than the front.

Combination frames use an acetate or TR90 front with metal temples. They photograph well, hit a middle price point, and carry a metal plaque logo cleanly.

Blue-light blocking and lens options

Blue-light frames ship with a blocking lens fitted as the demo lens unless you ask otherwise. The lens carries a light tint in most specifications, which is what makes the claim visible in the product photograph and in the hand; a completely clear block lens is available for buyers who prefer it, but it sells less well at retail because there is nothing to see.

For prescription supply, frames ship with clear demo lenses that the optician replaces. Tell us which you need at quoting, because it changes the landed cost and, on some shapes, the frame we would recommend.

Anti-reflective coating, hard coating and photochromic options can be quoted where the lens is supplied with the frame. Where the frame is going to an optical lab for glazing, we ship the frame with a demo lens and leave the lens choice to the lab.

Branding and finish

Optical branding tends to be quieter than sunglasses branding, and we run the same five methods to suit it: laser engraving, screen printing, hot foil stamping in gold or silver, a metal plaque, and heat transfer. On optical frames the usual choices are a small temple print or a laser mark, because a large mark on a thin temple looks wrong; a metal plaque suits combination frames where there is a front corner to mount it on. Inside-temple screen printing for model, size and power information is standard on request.

Colour is the main lever in optical. The same mould in black, tortoiseshell, crystal, matt navy or a translucent finish reads as several different products, and opticians buy by the colour card. We match frame colour to a Pantone reference or a physical sample.

Certification and documentation

CE and FDA documentation is available on request for this range. For optical supply the paperwork that matters most is usually the material declaration, particularly on nickel-containing metal frames, because the EU nickel release limit is the item opticians and importers get asked about. Ask for the declaration with your enquiry so it travels with the quotation rather than following the shipment.

Building an assortment that re-orders

Opticians re-order the frame that fits. So the practical assortment is built by fit rather than by trend: one wide fit, one standard, one narrow, in the shape that suits your market. Square and rectangle remain the safest core because they suit the widest range of faces and take a progressive lens without the wearer noticing the edge.

Then layer: a rimless or semi-rimless titanium option as the light-weight premium, a cat eye or butterfly for the women's segment, and a TR90 basic as the volume line. Blue-light versions of the two best-selling shapes are usually worth carrying as a separate SKU rather than as a variant.

Sampling and ordering

Send the reference plus the changes you want: frame colour, demo lens type, logo artwork and position, packaging. The quotation states the price at each quantity break and the minimum order quantity for that style. Samples come off the same moulds as production. Mixed orders across styles are normal, with each style clearing its own minimum.

Frequently asked

Answers cover the questions we are asked most about this range. Anything else, ask us directly.

Are these frames prescription ready?

Yes. Every optical frame in the range is designed to be glazed. Frames ship with clear demo lenses unless you specify a blue-light blocking lens.

Do the blue-light frames come with a tinted lens?

By default the blue-light frames ship with the blocking lens fitted, which usually carries a light tint. A clear blocking lens is available on request.

What is the minimum order quantity?

It is listed on each product page with the price at each quantity break, and differs by style. Stock moulds keep minimums low.

Can you print my logo on the temple?

Yes. We run laser engraving, screen printing, hot foil stamping in gold or silver, a metal plaque, and heat transfer. Inside-temple printing for model and size information is also available.

Which frame material is best for optical?

Acetate for premium feel and colour depth, TR90 for light and flexible everyday frames, metal and titanium where weight and a thin profile matter. The right choice depends on your price point and your customer's fitting needs.

Can I order a mixed assortment?

Yes, mixed orders are normal. Each style must clear its own minimum, and you can combine styles and colours in one shipment.

Can you match a frame colour from a sample?

Yes. Frame colour is matched to a Pantone reference or to a physical sample you send.

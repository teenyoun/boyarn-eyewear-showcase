What we produce

Reading glasses are the most predictable category in the eyewear trade and the least forgiving on quality: the buyer is often older, the frame is worn for hours at a time, and a hinge that fails turns a repeat customer into a complaint. Our readers run across the standard seven-step magnification range in acetate, metal and TR90, with folding and slim pocket styles for the travel and pharmacy channels.

Because the category is driven by assortment rather than by a single hero style, the practical way to buy it is a mixed-power set in one frame, plus one or two frames that read as a gift item.

Magnification and the seven-step range

Readers are stocked in +1.00, +1.50, +2.00, +2.50, +3.00, +3.50 and +4.00. That seven-step ladder covers almost every retail display from pharmacy to optician, and each step is a distinct SKU rather than a variant of the frame.

Demand is not evenly spread across the ladder. The middle of the range moves fastest and the ends are ordered in smaller quantities, so a first order built as an even split across all seven powers usually leaves you long on the extremes. Ask for a weighted split and we will quote it that way. Above +4.00 the lens curvature starts to push the frame geometry, so those powers need a specific frame rather than any frame in the range.

Lens and construction

Reader lenses are optical-grade acrylic or resin, cut to the specified power and fitted in the workshop. Anti-reflective and hard coatings can be quoted. Where the reader is meant for screen use, a blue-light blocking version can be specified, though the more common request at this price point is a straightforward reader with a clean, scratch-resistant surface.

Spring hinges are worth specifying on any reader sold at retail. They absorb the opening force that kills a barrel hinge, and they are the difference between a reader that lasts a year and one that lasts a month. Folding readers trade some rigidity for pocket size and suit travel and gift channels.

Branding and retail packaging

Reader branding is usually kept light. We run laser engraving, screen printing, hot foil stamping in gold or silver, a metal plaque, and heat transfer; on readers the common pick is a small temple print or a laser mark, plus the power marking on the inside temple, which is a retail requirement in most markets. Packaging does more work in this category than in any other eyewear category, because the reader is often bought as a gift or an impulse purchase. A printed sleeve, a hard case and a cleaning cloth carrying the same logo is the standard retail build.

Shelf presentation matters too: a counter display carrying several powers in one frame is the normal way this category is sold in pharmacy and travel retail, and it can be branded with your logo.

Certification

CE and FDA documentation is available on request for the reader range. Because readers are sold at retail and often cross borders as consumer products rather than as optical goods, the paperwork you need depends on the destination market, so tell us where the order is landing and we quote the correct set with it.

Building a reader assortment

Start from one frame that fits well across the whole ladder, then take the full seven powers in that frame with a weighted split favouring the middle. Add a second frame in a different material or colour so your display shows choice, and one folding or slim style for the pocket and gift channels. Beyond that, add blue-light or sun-reader versions only if your customer base asks for them, because each addition multiplies the SKU count across seven powers.

Quality points that decide re-orders

Four things decide whether a reader comes back. The hinge, covered above. The lens surface, which scuffs if the coating is thin and will be the first complaint you hear about. The power accuracy, because a reader marked +2.00 that measures +1.75 is a return rather than a sale. And the temple tips, which crack first on cheap acetate and are the cheapest thing on the frame to specify properly. None of these are visible in a product photograph, which is why buying readers on image alone is how buyers end up with a container they cannot sell through.

Sampling and ordering

Send the catalogue reference and the powers you want, plus logo artwork, packaging and display requirements. The quotation states the price at each quantity break and the minimum order quantity for that style. Mixed orders combining several frames and powers are normal, and each style must clear its own minimum.

Frequently asked

Answers cover the questions we are asked most about this range. Anything else, ask us directly.

What powers do you stock?

The standard seven-step range: +1.00, +1.50, +2.00, +2.50, +3.00, +3.50 and +4.00. Each power is a separate SKU.

Can you supply an uneven split across powers?

Yes. Demand concentrates in the middle of the ladder, so a weighted split usually sells through better than an even one. Tell us the split you want and it is quoted that way.

Do the readers have spring hinges?

Spring hinges are available and are recommended on any reader sold at retail, because they survive the opening force that wears out a standard barrel hinge.

Can I put my logo on the reader and the packaging?

Yes. Laser engraving, screen printing, hot foil stamping in gold or silver, a metal plaque and heat transfer are all available on the frame, and the same logo can be printed on the case, pouch, cleaning cloth or display.

Are the powers marked on the frame?

Yes. The power is marked on the inside temple, which is a retail requirement in most markets.

Do you make folding reading glasses?

Yes, folding and slim pocket styles are part of the range and suit travel and gift channels.

Can I combine reading glasses with other styles in one order?

Yes, mixed orders are normal across the catalogue. Each style must clear its own minimum order quantity.

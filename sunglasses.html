What we produce

Our sunglasses line covers the shapes that sell in volume for importers, online sellers and private label brands. Square and rectangle frames make up the core of the range because they carry the widest size and fit tolerance, follow the strongest demand across Europe and North America, and take printed and engraved branding well. Around that core we hold rimless and semi-rimless styles, small metal frames for narrow fits, round and oval retro shapes, cat eye and butterfly silhouettes for the women's segment, plus wrap and shield shapes for sports and driving.

Every style is a stock mould we already run, which is why minimums stay low and sampling is fast. You are not paying for a new tool, you are choosing a frame, a lens, a colour and a logo position.

Frame materials and construction

Acetate frames are cut and polished from sheet stock, then barrel-tumbled and hand-finished. Acetate accepts deep colour, tortoiseshell and translucent finishes that injection plastics cannot reproduce, and it can be heat-adjusted at the optician, which matters for higher price points. The trade-off is weight and cost per piece.

TR90 is the workhorse for light, flexible frames. It survives being sat on, returns to shape, and keeps a matt finish that hides handling marks, which is why it dominates sports and children's styles. PC frames sit below TR90 on cost and are the practical choice for promotional and entry price points.

Metal frames are built on stainless steel or alloy wire with adjustable nose arms and acetate or silicone temple tips. Titanium appears where weight is the selling point, typically in rimless and small round styles. Hinge choice matters more than most buyers expect: a standard barrel hinge is cheap and serviceable, a spring hinge survives being opened past its limit and is worth the small premium on anything sold at retail.

Lens options

UV400 is the default across the range: the lens blocks UVA and UVB across the full spectrum and is the claim most export markets require you to be able to document. Polarised lenses are the next step up. A polarising film, usually TAC, cuts glare reflected off water, snow and road surface, which is why polarised sells hardest into driving, fishing and beach retail.

Beyond protection, the finish does the selling. Gradient tint fades from dark at the brow to light at the cheek and photographs well; mirrored coatings give the high-contrast look the fashion segment wants; photochromic lenses darken in daylight and clear indoors, which suits the driving and prescription-over-sunglasses market. Thickness is specifiable, and we can match a tint to a reference sample rather than a swatch code.

Branding, colour and packaging

We run five branding methods: laser engraving, screen printing, hot foil stamping in gold or silver, a metal plaque, and heat transfer. Each has a different job. Laser engraving cuts a permanent tonal mark that cannot rub off, and is the default on acetate and coated metal temples. Screen printing lays an opaque colour with a hard, sharp edge, which is what you want for a solid logo on a flat temple face. Hot foil stamping in gold or silver gives a metallic mark without the cost of a separate metal part, and reads as premium on acetate. A metal plaque is a bonded part rather than a surface treatment, so it is the most durable of the five and the usual choice on the higher price points. Heat transfer carries multi-colour artwork and fine detail, and takes graphics that screen printing cannot hold.

Position and size are settled at sampling, because moving a logo after the mould trim is fixed usually costs more than choosing correctly the first time.

Frame colour is matched to Pantone references or to a physical sample. Lens colour runs independently of frame colour, so a black frame with a grey-green lens and the same frame with a brown gradient are two SKUs from one style. Packaging runs from a plain polybag through a soft pouch and a hard case to a printed box; the case and box can carry the same logo as the frame.

Choosing a range for your market

A workable starting assortment is usually built around shape and lens rather than around colour. Take one strong square in metal, one in acetate, a rimless or semi-rimless option, a small or narrow fit for the Asian and youth market, and a cat eye for the women's segment. Then decide lens treatment per style rather than per range: polarised on the styles you expect to sell into driving and outdoor retail, gradient and mirror on the fashion styles.

If you are selling online, resist the temptation to list every colour of every shape. Buyers compare images, so a tight set of well-shot styles converts better than a wide set shot badly, and the second order is what pays for the first.

Certification and testing

Our sunglasses are CE marked and FDA compliant, and the supporting documentation is available on request with a quotation. If you are importing into a market that audits UV claims at the border, ask for the paperwork up front rather than at shipment, because the lead time to issue it sits outside production. Lens transmission, impact resistance and material declarations are the three items buyers ask for most often, and all three can be supplied for the style you are ordering.

Sampling and ordering

Send us the catalogue reference and the changes you want: frame colour, lens colour and treatment, logo artwork and position, packaging. We come back with a quotation that states the price at each quantity break and the minimum order quantity for that style, because those differ by frame. Samples are built from the same moulds as production, so what you approve is what ships.

Production runs from the confirmed sample. Mixed containers are normal for this category: you can combine several styles and colours in one order as long as each style clears its own minimum.

Frequently asked

Answers cover the questions we are asked most about this range. Anything else, ask us directly.

What is the minimum order quantity?

It varies by style and is listed on each product page together with the price at each quantity break. Stock moulds keep minimums low, typically a few pieces per colour for ready-stock lines and higher for a full custom colour run.

Can you put my logo on the frame?

Yes. We run five methods: laser engraving, screen printing, hot foil stamping in gold or silver, a metal plaque, and heat transfer. Position and size are confirmed at sampling so the mark sits correctly on the temple or lens corner.

Are your sunglasses CE marked and FDA compliant?

Yes, and the documentation is available on request. Ask for it with your enquiry if you need it for customs or for your own compliance file.

Are the lenses UV400?

UV400 is the default lens across the sunglasses range. Polarised, gradient, mirrored and photochromic treatments are available as options and are noted per style.

Do you make polarised sunglasses?

Yes, polarised lenses are a standard option, generally using TAC polarising film. Polarised styles are the usual choice for driving, fishing and beach retail.

Can I mix styles and colours in one order?

Yes, mixed orders are normal. Each style has to clear its own minimum order quantity, but you can combine several styles and colours in a single shipment.

What frame materials do you offer?

Acetate, TR90, PC, stainless steel and alloy metal, and titanium on selected rimless and light-weight styles. Each material is listed on the product page.

Can you match a colour I send you?

Yes. Frame colour can be matched to a Pantone reference or to a physical sample, and lens tint can be matched to a reference rather than a swatch code.

How long does sampling take?

Sampling is fast because every style is an existing mould rather than a new tool. Confirmed timing is quoted with your enquiry, since it depends on the logo method and whether a custom colour run is involved.
